Bakery-Style Blueberry Muffins

Bake soft blueberry muffins with a tender crumb, juicy berries, a hint of lemon, and a golden domed top using simple pantry ingredients.

Prep: 15 minCook: 22 minServes: 12 muffins
🫐

Recipe at a glance: 37 minutes · 12 muffins · Baking · Primary keyword: bakery style blueberry muffins.

Bakery-style blueberry muffins should have a soft crumb, bursts of fruit, and a rounded top. The batter is intentionally mixed by hand and left slightly lumpy. Smooth, overmixed muffin batter often produces a tougher texture because excess mixing develops gluten in the flour.

A little lemon zest makes the berries taste brighter without turning the muffins into a strongly citrus-flavored bake. Melted butter brings richness, while baking powder creates lift. A short initial bake at a higher temperature encourages the tops to rise, then a lower temperature finishes the centers without over-browning.

Fresh or frozen blueberries both work. Frozen berries should go into the batter directly from the freezer to reduce color streaking, and the bake may take a little longer. Fill the cups evenly so they finish at the same time, and cool the muffins briefly before moving them to a rack.

This method separates the steps that build flavor from the steps that protect texture. Browning, aromatics, liquid, and finishing ingredients each have a clear role. Ingredients

Prepare everything before turning on the stove. The ingredient list below matches the quantities used in the recipe instructions and structured recipe data.

  • 250 g all-purpose flour (2 cups)
  • 150 g granulated sugar (3/4 cup)
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 180 ml milk (3/4 cup)
  • 115 g unsalted butter, melted and cooled (1/2 cup)
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tsp finely grated lemon zest
  • 200 g blueberries, fresh or frozen (about 1 1/3 cups)
  • 1 tbsp flour, for tossing berries if desired
  • 1 tbsp coarse sugar for tops, optional

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Heat oven to 200°C / 400°F. Line a 12-cup muffin pan with paper liners or grease each well thoroughly.
  2. Whisk flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt in a large bowl. In a separate bowl, whisk eggs, milk, cooled melted butter, vanilla, and lemon zest.
  3. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients. Fold gently until the flour is nearly incorporated; the batter should look slightly lumpy, not smooth.
  4. Fold in blueberries with only a few strokes. If using frozen berries, add them directly from the freezer to reduce streaking.
  5. Divide batter among 12 muffin cups, filling each about three-quarters full. Sprinkle coarse sugar over the tops if desired.
  6. Bake at 200°C / 400°F for 5 minutes, then reduce the oven to 180°C / 350°F without opening the door and bake another 14–18 minutes, until domed and a tester comes out clean.
  7. Cool in the pan for 5 minutes, then transfer muffins to a rack. Let them cool enough to set before serving.

Chef's tips for reliable results

  • A brief hotter start helps muffins rise into domed tops; avoid opening the oven during the first 10 minutes.
  • Do not overmix. Overworked batter develops more gluten and can make muffins tough.
  • If using frozen berries, do not thaw them first.
  • Use room-temperature eggs and milk for a more even batter and predictable bake.

Easy variations and substitutions

  • Replace lemon zest with orange zest for a softer citrus note.
  • Add 50 g white chocolate chips or a little cinnamon for a different flavor profile.
  • For a crumb topping, rub 30 g flour, 25 g sugar, and 20 g cold butter together and sprinkle over the batter.

Storage, reheating, and make-ahead notes

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping preparation: measure ingredients and complete chopping before cooking starts, especially for recipes that use high heat.
  • Overcrowding the pan: too many ingredients at once release moisture and can prevent browning. Work in batches if necessary.
  • Ignoring texture cues: use the suggested times as a guide, then check doneness, color, and consistency.
  • Seasoning only at the beginning: taste at the end and adjust salt, pepper, herbs, and acidity in small increments.
  • Storing food while very hot in a deep container: divide larger quantities into shallow containers so they cool more quickly and safely.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use frozen blueberries?

Yes. Fold them in frozen and expect a little extra baking time if the batter is very cold.

Why did my muffins turn out dense?

Overmixing, old baking powder, or too much flour can prevent a light crumb.

How should I store blueberry muffins?

Store fully cooled muffins in a loosely covered container at room temperature for about 2 days, or freeze for longer storage.
