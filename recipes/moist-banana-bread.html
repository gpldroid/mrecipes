Moist Banana Bread

This moist banana bread recipe uses very ripe bananas, melted butter, and a simple one-bowl method for a tender loaf with a golden crust and rich banana flavor.

Prep: 15 minCook: 55 minServes: 1 loaf (10 slices)
🍌

Recipe at a glance: 70 minutes · 1 loaf (10 slices) · Baking · Primary keyword: moist banana bread recipe.

A good banana bread should be tender and moist without becoming gummy or heavy. The secret is using very ripe fruit, measuring flour carefully, and mixing the batter only until the dry ingredients disappear. This straightforward loaf needs no mixer, making it useful for breakfast, snacks, or a simple homemade dessert.

Bananas provide sweetness, moisture, and flavor, while melted butter gives the crumb richness. The egg helps the loaf set, and baking soda reacts with the banana's acidity to create lift. Because banana size varies, weighing the peeled fruit is more reliable than counting bananas alone.

The loaf browns before the center is always finished, so begin checking near the lower end of the baking window. A skewer should show moist crumbs rather than wet batter. Let the bread cool before slicing; the crumb continues to set outside the oven, and a hot loaf can seem underbaked even when it is done.

Why this moist banana bread recipe works

This method separates the steps that build flavor from the steps that protect texture. Browning, aromatics, liquid, and finishing ingredients each have a clear role. Follow the order and use the sensory cues as well as the timer: ingredients vary in size, pans retain heat differently, and stovetops are not calibrated alike.

The quantities are provided in metric measurements with common US equivalents where useful. A kitchen scale gives the most consistent results, especially for flour, pasta, and produce. Salt brands and broth concentrations differ, so taste near the end and adjust gradually rather than adding extra seasoning automatically.

Ingredients

Prepare everything before turning on the stove. The ingredient list below matches the quantities used in the recipe instructions and structured recipe data.

  • 3 very ripe medium bananas, mashed (about 330 g peeled)
  • 115 g unsalted butter, melted (1/2 cup)
  • 150 g granulated sugar (3/4 cup), or 120 g for less sweetness
  • 1 large egg, lightly beaten
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract (5 ml)
  • 190 g all-purpose flour (1 1/2 cups)
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon, optional
  • 80 g chopped walnuts or chocolate chips, optional

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Heat oven to 175°C / 350°F. Grease a 9 x 5-inch (23 x 13 cm) loaf pan and line it with a parchment sling for easy removal.
  2. Mash the bananas in a large bowl until mostly smooth, leaving a few small pieces for texture.
  3. Whisk in melted butter, sugar, egg, and vanilla until combined. Let the butter cool slightly first so it does not scramble the egg.
  4. Sprinkle flour, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon over the wet mixture. Fold gently with a spatula only until no dry flour remains; overmixing can make the loaf tough.
  5. Fold in nuts or chocolate chips if using. Transfer batter to the prepared pan and smooth the top.
  6. Bake for 50–60 minutes, checking at 50 minutes. A skewer inserted in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Tent loosely with foil if the top browns too quickly.
  7. Cool in the pan for 10–15 minutes, then lift onto a rack and cool further before slicing. The crumb sets as it cools, so cutting too soon can make it seem gummy.

Chef's tips for reliable results

  • Very ripe bananas with plenty of brown speckles provide the best flavor and moisture.
  • Measure flour by weight if possible; too much flour is a common cause of dry banana bread.
  • Mix just until combined. A few lumps are fine and preferable to overworking the batter.
  • If your bananas are smaller, weigh the peeled fruit rather than relying only on the number of bananas.

Easy variations and substitutions

  • Add chopped toasted walnuts for crunch or chocolate chips for a dessert-like loaf.
  • Replace up to 50 g flour with whole-wheat flour for a slightly nuttier crumb.
  • Add 1 tablespoon plain yogurt if your bananas are not especially ripe, but avoid making the batter overly wet.

What to serve with it

Choose sides that complement the dish instead of competing with it. A crisp green salad, steamed or roasted seasonal vegetables, or simply prepared grains can balance a rich main course. For pasta and soup, warm bread is useful for the sauce; for breakfast dishes and baking, serve with fresh fruit, yogurt, or a drink of choice. Add acidic elements such as lemon or lime at the table when the finished dish needs brightness.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping preparation: measure ingredients and complete chopping before cooking starts, especially for recipes that use high heat.
  • Overcrowding the pan: too many ingredients at once release moisture and can prevent browning. Work in batches if necessary.
  • Ignoring texture cues: use the suggested times as a guide, then check doneness, color, and consistency.
  • Seasoning only at the beginning: taste at the end and adjust salt, pepper, herbs, and acidity in small increments.
  • Storing food while very hot in a deep container: divide larger quantities into shallow containers so they cool more quickly and safely.

Frequently asked questions

How ripe should bananas be?

Choose bananas with brown-speckled skins and soft flesh; very green bananas are less sweet and harder to mash.

Why is my banana bread gummy?

It may be underbaked, overmixed, or sliced while still hot. Check the center and cool before cutting.

Can I freeze banana bread?

Yes. Cool completely, wrap slices or the whole loaf tightly, and freeze for up to about 3 months for best quality.

Planning, texture, and serving notes

Banana bread is a useful recipe for ripe fruit, but fruit moisture varies. If the bananas are exceptionally large or watery, the loaf may need a few extra minutes in the oven. If they are small, weigh the peeled fruit and avoid adding extra liquid to compensate. The batter should be thick and scoopable, not pour like pancake batter. Small lumps of banana are normal and add pockets of flavor to the finished crumb.

Pan material can change the bake. A dark metal pan usually browns the edges faster than a light-colored pan, while glass may require a slightly different timing. Begin checking before the full baking window ends and use the center as the deciding point. If the top is dark but the center is not ready, tent loosely with foil and continue baking. Let the loaf cool in the pan briefly, then transfer it to a rack so the bottom does not trap steam.

For neat slices, use a serrated knife and wait until the loaf is mostly cool. Plain slices are excellent with coffee or tea, while a small amount of yogurt or nut butter can turn one into a more substantial snack. Store the cooled loaf wrapped so it does not dry out. For freezing, slice first and separate pieces with parchment; individual slices thaw faster and make portioning easy.
