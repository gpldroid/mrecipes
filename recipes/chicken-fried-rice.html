Easy Chicken Fried Rice

Make takeout-style chicken fried rice at home with tender chicken, fluffy rice, egg, vegetables, and a savory soy-sesame seasoning in one hot pan.

Prep: 15 minCook: 15 minServes: 4 servings
🍚

Recipe at a glance: 30 minutes · 4 servings · Dinner · Primary keyword: easy chicken fried rice.

Easy chicken fried rice turns cooked rice into a satisfying meal with very little waste. It is a practical way to use leftovers, but good fried rice is more than rice mixed with soy sauce. High heat, a wide pan, small pieces of chicken, and relatively dry grains help create distinct, lightly toasted rice rather than a damp pile.

Day-old rice is ideal because refrigeration dries the surface and makes the grains easier to separate. If you are cooking rice specifically for this dish, spread it in a shallow layer and cool it promptly. Cook the chicken first, then the egg and vegetables, and add the rice once the pan is hot again.

Soy sauce provides salt and umami, while a small amount of sesame oil contributes aroma. Sesame oil is best added near the end so its fragrance remains noticeable. Taste before adding extra salt, and keep the ingredient pieces small enough that each serving gets a little chicken, egg, and vegetable.

Why this easy chicken fried rice recipe works

This method separates the steps that build flavor from the steps that protect texture. Browning, aromatics, liquid, and finishing ingredients each have a clear role. Follow the order and use the sensory cues as well as the timer: ingredients vary in size, pans retain heat differently, and stovetops are not calibrated alike.

The quantities are provided in metric measurements with common US equivalents where useful. A kitchen scale gives the most consistent results, especially for flour, pasta, and produce. Salt brands and broth concentrations differ, so taste near the end and adjust gradually rather than adding extra seasoning automatically.

Ingredients

Prepare everything before turning on the stove. The ingredient list below matches the quantities used in the recipe instructions and structured recipe data.

  • 600 g cold cooked rice (about 4 cups), preferably day-old
  • 300 g boneless chicken breast, cut into small cubes (10 oz)
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil, divided (30 ml)
  • 2 large eggs, lightly beaten
  • 150 g frozen peas and carrots (about 1 cup)
  • 3 scallions, sliced, whites and greens separated
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce (30 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp ground white or black pepper
  • Salt, only if needed
  • Optional: 1 tsp grated fresh ginger

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Break up cold rice with clean hands or a fork, separating clumps. If using freshly cooked rice, spread it on a tray and cool quickly; avoid leaving cooked rice at room temperature for extended periods.
  2. Pat the chicken dry and season lightly with pepper. Heat 1 tablespoon neutral oil in a large wok or skillet over high heat.
  3. Add chicken in one layer and stir-fry for 4–6 minutes until browned and cooked through to 74°C / 165°F. Transfer to a clean plate.
  4. Add the remaining oil. Pour in the eggs and scramble for 30–45 seconds until just set, then push them to the side of the pan.
  5. Add scallion whites, garlic, and ginger if using. Stir-fry for 20–30 seconds, then add peas and carrots and cook until hot.
  6. Add rice and spread it across the pan. Let it sit briefly between tosses so some grains toast rather than steam. Break apart any remaining clumps.
  7. Return chicken to the pan. Add soy sauce around the hot edge of the wok, toss well, then finish with sesame oil and scallion greens. Taste before adding salt because soy sauce is already salty.

Chef's tips for reliable results

  • Cold, relatively dry rice gives separate grains; freshly steamed rice tends to clump.
  • Cook in batches if your pan is small. Crowding lowers the temperature and makes the rice soggy.
  • Prepare all ingredients before heating the wok because the cooking process moves quickly.
  • Refrigerate leftover rice promptly; do not leave cooked rice sitting out for hours.

Easy variations and substitutions

  • Replace chicken with firm tofu, shrimp, or extra vegetables.
  • Add diced bell pepper or finely shredded cabbage for crunch.
  • For a spicy version, serve with chili crisp or add a small amount of chili-garlic sauce.

What to serve with it

Choose sides that complement the dish instead of competing with it. A crisp green salad, steamed or roasted seasonal vegetables, or simply prepared grains can balance a rich main course. For pasta and soup, warm bread is useful for the sauce; for breakfast dishes and baking, serve with fresh fruit, yogurt, or a drink of choice. Add acidic elements such as lemon or lime at the table when the finished dish needs brightness.

Storage, reheating, and make-ahead notes

Cool leftovers promptly and refrigerate them in a clean, covered container. As a general guideline, cooked leftovers should be used within 3–4 days, unless an ingredient-specific food-safety recommendation calls for a shorter period. Reheat only the portion you plan to eat, until thoroughly hot. If the dish thickens in the refrigerator, add a small splash of water or broth while reheating. Do not leave perishable food at room temperature for extended periods.

For meal prep, measure dry seasonings and chop sturdy vegetables in advance. Keep raw meat or seafood separate from ready-to-eat ingredients, wash hands and utensils after handling them, and thaw frozen ingredients safely in the refrigerator or under cold running water. For best texture, add delicate herbs, greens, crunchy toppings, or final citrus just before serving.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping preparation: measure ingredients and complete chopping before cooking starts, especially for recipes that use high heat.
  • Overcrowding the pan: too many ingredients at once release moisture and can prevent browning. Work in batches if necessary.
  • Ignoring texture cues: use the suggested times as a guide, then check doneness, color, and consistency.
  • Seasoning only at the beginning: taste at the end and adjust salt, pepper, herbs, and acidity in small increments.
  • Storing food while very hot in a deep container: divide larger quantities into shallow containers so they cool more quickly and safely.

Frequently asked questions

What rice works best?

Long-grain rice such as jasmine is a good choice because it tends to remain separate when cold.

Can I use freshly cooked rice?

Yes, but cool it quickly in a shallow layer and let excess steam escape before stir-frying.

Can I freeze fried rice?

It can be frozen in portions, but cool and refrigerate or freeze promptly and reheat thoroughly.
