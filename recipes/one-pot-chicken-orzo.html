One-Pot Chicken Orzo

Make one-pot chicken orzo with tender chicken, lemon, spinach, and a creamy, savory orzo pasta base. This easy weeknight dinner uses one pan and is ready in about 35 minutes.

Prep: 10 minCook: 25 minServes: 4 servings

Recipe at a glance: 35 minutes · 4 servings · Dinner · Primary keyword: one pot chicken orzo.

One-pot chicken orzo is designed for the evenings when you want a complete dinner but do not want to manage several pans. Orzo looks like rice, but it is a small pasta shape; it cooks directly in broth and releases starch that gives the finished dish a naturally silky texture.

Chicken, lemon, tomatoes, spinach, and Parmesan bring a useful balance of savory, fresh, and creamy flavors. The lemon is not simply a garnish: its acidity lifts the broth and keeps the cheese from making the dish taste heavy. This is a flexible weeknight recipe, but the order of cooking matters because the chicken needs browning and the orzo needs enough liquid to soften.

The method begins by browning the chicken, then building flavor in the same pan. Those browned bits dissolve into the broth, giving the orzo a deeper taste than it would have if everything were added at once. Keep the heat at a steady simmer rather than a hard boil, and stir more often as the liquid reduces.

Why this one-pot chicken orzo recipe works

This method separates the steps that build flavor from the steps that protect texture. Browning, aromatics, liquid, and finishing ingredients each have a clear role. Follow the order and use the sensory cues as well as the timer: ingredients vary in size, pans retain heat differently, and stovetops are not calibrated alike.

The quantities are provided in metric measurements with common US equivalents where useful. A kitchen scale gives the most consistent results, especially for flour, pasta, and produce. Salt brands and broth concentrations differ, so taste near the end and adjust gradually rather than adding extra seasoning automatically.

Ingredients

Prepare everything before turning on the stove. The ingredient list below matches the quantities used in the recipe instructions and structured recipe data.

  • 600 g boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into bite-size pieces (about 1 1/3 lb)
  • 250 g dry orzo (about 1 1/4 cups)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil (15 ml)
  • 1 small yellow onion, finely diced (about 120 g)
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 750 ml low-sodium chicken broth (3 1/4 cups)
  • 150 g cherry tomatoes, halved (1 cup)
  • 100 g baby spinach (about 3 packed cups)
  • 40 g finely grated Parmesan (about 1/2 cup)
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1/2 tsp sweet paprika
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice, plus zest to taste
  • 3/4 tsp fine salt, or to taste
  • 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Pat the chicken dry. Season it with the salt, pepper, paprika, and oregano so the surface browns rather than steams.
  2. Heat the olive oil in a wide, deep skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the chicken in one layer and brown for 3–4 minutes, turning once. It does not need to be fully cooked yet. Transfer to a plate.
  3. Lower the heat to medium. Add the onion and cook for 3 minutes, stirring and scraping up browned bits. Add the garlic and cook for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  4. Stir in the dry orzo and toast for 60 seconds. Pour in the broth, add the tomatoes, and return the chicken and any juices to the pan. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  5. Cover partially and cook for 10–12 minutes, stirring every 2–3 minutes so the orzo does not stick. Add a splash of hot water if the pan becomes dry before the pasta is tender.
  6. Check that the chicken reaches 74°C / 165°F in the thickest pieces. Fold in the spinach until wilted, then stir in the Parmesan, lemon juice, and a little lemon zest.
  7. Rest off the heat for 2 minutes. Taste and adjust salt, pepper, lemon, or Parmesan. Serve warm while the sauce is glossy.

Chef's tips for reliable results

  • Use a wide pan so the orzo cooks evenly and liquid reduces at a steady rate.
  • Stir frequently near the end; orzo releases starch and can settle on the bottom.
  • Chicken thighs stay juicy, but boneless breasts also work if cut evenly and checked for doneness.
  • Add spinach at the end to keep its color fresh. Frozen spinach should be thawed and squeezed dry first.

Easy variations and substitutions

  • For Mediterranean flavor, add sliced Kalamata olives and crumbled feta just before serving.
  • For extra vegetables, add diced zucchini with the onion or peas during the final 3 minutes.
  • For a richer finish, stir in 2 tablespoons of cream; for dairy-free, omit Parmesan or use a suitable alternative.

What to serve with it

Choose sides that complement the dish instead of competing with it. A crisp green salad, steamed or roasted seasonal vegetables, or simply prepared grains can balance a rich main course. For pasta and soup, warm bread is useful for the sauce; for breakfast dishes and baking, serve with fresh fruit, yogurt, or a drink of choice. Add acidic elements such as lemon or lime at the table when the finished dish needs brightness.

Storage, reheating, and make-ahead notes

Cool leftovers promptly and refrigerate them in a clean, covered container. As a general guideline, cooked leftovers should be used within 3–4 days, unless an ingredient-specific food-safety recommendation calls for a shorter period. Reheat only the portion you plan to eat, until thoroughly hot. If the dish thickens in the refrigerator, add a small splash of water or broth while reheating. Do not leave perishable food at room temperature for extended periods.

For meal prep, measure dry seasonings and chop sturdy vegetables in advance. Keep raw meat or seafood separate from ready-to-eat ingredients, wash hands and utensils after handling them, and thaw frozen ingredients safely in the refrigerator or under cold running water. For best texture, add delicate herbs, greens, crunchy toppings, or final citrus just before serving.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping preparation: measure ingredients and complete chopping before cooking starts, especially for recipes that use high heat.
  • Overcrowding the pan: too many ingredients at once release moisture and can prevent browning. Work in batches if necessary.
  • Ignoring texture cues: use the suggested times as a guide, then check doneness, color, and consistency.
  • Seasoning only at the beginning: taste at the end and adjust salt, pepper, herbs, and acidity in small increments.
  • Storing food while very hot in a deep container: divide larger quantities into shallow containers so they cool more quickly and safely.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make chicken orzo ahead?

It is best freshly cooked, but leftovers keep well. The orzo absorbs liquid as it sits, so loosen it with broth when reheating.

Can I use a different pasta?

Small pasta shapes can work, but cooking time and liquid needs vary. Check the package and add broth gradually.

How do I know the chicken is cooked?

Use a food thermometer and check that the thickest pieces reach 74°C / 165°F.

Planning, texture, and serving notes

To plan this meal around a busy evening, measure the orzo and broth first, slice the tomatoes, and keep the spinach and Parmesan near the stove. Once the liquid is simmering, the pasta needs attention because starch can settle quickly on the bottom of a pan. Stir with a wooden spoon that reaches the corners, and check the texture a minute or two before the package time suggests. The ideal finish is tender pasta with a little loose sauce, not a dry casserole.

Ingredient size affects timing more than many cooks expect. Small chicken pieces brown quickly and finish at the same time as the orzo; large chunks may need to be seared longer or cut down before cooking. If the broth is absorbed while the pasta is still firm, add hot liquid in small splashes rather than pouring in a large amount at once. If the dish looks too loose, simmer uncovered briefly and stir so the bottom does not catch.

For a balanced plate, serve the orzo with a crisp cucumber salad or simply dressed greens. The dish already contains protein, pasta, and spinach, so an elaborate side is unnecessary. Leftovers make a useful lunch, but the texture will thicken overnight. Reheat slowly with broth, stirring until the sauce loosens and the chicken is hot throughout. Add a fresh squeeze of lemon after reheating to restore the bright finish.
