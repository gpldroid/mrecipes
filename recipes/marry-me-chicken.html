Marry Me Chicken

This marry me chicken recipe pairs golden seared chicken with a creamy sun-dried tomato Parmesan sauce, garlic, and herbs for a restaurant-style dinner made at home.

Prep: 12 minCook: 25 minServes: 4 servings

Recipe at a glance: 37 minutes · 4 servings · Dinner · Primary keyword: marry me chicken recipe.

Marry me chicken is a modern comfort-food favorite built around golden chicken and a creamy sun-dried tomato sauce. The appeal is contrast: browned meat, tangy tomatoes, savory Parmesan, aromatic garlic, and fresh basil all meet in one skillet. The name is playful rather than a claim about the dish's historical origin.

This version keeps the ingredient list practical and the sauce balanced. Sun-dried tomatoes provide concentrated flavor, so a modest amount goes a long way. Chicken broth loosens the pan fond, while cream softens the acidity and Parmesan adds saltiness and body. Fresh basil at the end keeps the finished dish from tasting one-dimensional.

For even cooking, make the chicken pieces a similar thickness. A quick sear develops color; the chicken then finishes gently in the sauce. Avoid boiling the cream aggressively, and use a thermometer rather than relying on appearance alone. The result is well suited to pasta, rice, mashed potatoes, or bread that can catch the sauce.

This method separates the steps that build flavor from the steps that protect texture. Browning, aromatics, liquid, and finishing ingredients each have a clear role. Ingredients

Prepare everything before turning on the stove. The ingredient list below matches the quantities used in the recipe instructions and structured recipe data.

  • 4 boneless, skinless chicken breasts, about 680 g (1 1/2 lb)
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 2 tbsp olive oil (30 ml)
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 60 g sun-dried tomatoes in oil, drained and sliced (about 1/2 cup)
  • 180 ml low-sodium chicken broth (3/4 cup)
  • 240 ml heavy cream (1 cup)
  • 50 g finely grated Parmesan (about 1/2 cup)
  • 1/2 tsp crushed red pepper flakes, optional
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh basil, plus more to serve
  • 1 tsp cornstarch mixed with 1 tbsp cold water, optional

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Pat the chicken dry. If the breasts are thick, slice them horizontally or gently pound to an even thickness. Season both sides with salt, pepper, and Italian seasoning.
  2. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Sear chicken for 4–6 minutes per side, depending on thickness, until golden. Transfer to a plate; it will finish cooking in the sauce.
  3. Reduce heat to medium. Add garlic and sun-dried tomatoes and stir for 30–45 seconds, taking care not to burn the garlic.
  4. Pour in the chicken broth and scrape the pan to release browned flavor. Simmer for 2 minutes to reduce slightly.
  5. Lower the heat. Stir in the cream, Parmesan, and red pepper flakes if using. Keep the sauce at a gentle simmer; vigorous boiling can make the dairy split or reduce too quickly.
  6. Return chicken and any accumulated juices to the skillet. Simmer gently for 5–8 minutes, turning once, until the thickest part reaches 74°C / 165°F.
  7. If you prefer a thicker sauce, stir in the cornstarch slurry a little at a time and simmer for 1 minute. Finish with fresh basil and serve with pasta, mashed potatoes, or crusty bread.

Chef's tips for reliable results

  • Dry the chicken before searing to encourage a golden crust.
  • Use oil-packed sun-dried tomatoes for deeper flavor; drain them so the sauce is not overly oily.
  • Grate Parmesan finely so it melts smoothly. Add it over low heat rather than boiling the sauce.
  • Chicken thickness determines cooking time, so temperature is more reliable than the clock.

Easy variations and substitutions

  • Add baby spinach during the final 2 minutes for a green vegetable in the sauce.
  • Use boneless chicken thighs for a forgiving, juicy alternative; allow a little extra time if thick.
  • For a lighter sauce, substitute half-and-half, understanding that it will be less rich and may need gentler heat.

What to serve with it

Choose sides that complement the dish instead of competing with it. A crisp green salad, steamed or roasted seasonal vegetables, or simply prepared grains can balance a rich main course. For pasta and soup, warm bread is useful for the sauce; for breakfast dishes and baking, serve with fresh fruit, yogurt, or a drink of choice. Add acidic elements such as lemon or lime at the table when the finished dish needs brightness.

Storage, reheating, and make-ahead notes

Cool leftovers promptly and refrigerate them in a clean, covered container. As a general guideline, cooked leftovers should be used within 3–4 days, unless an ingredient-specific food-safety recommendation calls for a shorter period. Reheat only the portion you plan to eat, until thoroughly hot. If the dish thickens in the refrigerator, add a small splash of water or broth while reheating. Do not leave perishable food at room temperature for extended periods.

For meal prep, measure dry seasonings and chop sturdy vegetables in advance. Keep raw meat or seafood separate from ready-to-eat ingredients, wash hands and utensils after handling them, and thaw frozen ingredients safely in the refrigerator or under cold running water. For best texture, add delicate herbs, greens, crunchy toppings, or final citrus just before serving.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping preparation: measure ingredients and complete chopping before cooking starts, especially for recipes that use high heat.
  • Overcrowding the pan: too many ingredients at once release moisture and can prevent browning. Work in batches if necessary.
  • Ignoring texture cues: use the suggested times as a guide, then check doneness, color, and consistency.
  • Seasoning only at the beginning: taste at the end and adjust salt, pepper, herbs, and acidity in small increments.
  • Storing food while very hot in a deep container: divide larger quantities into shallow containers so they cool more quickly and safely.

Frequently asked questions

Why is it called marry me chicken?

The playful name refers to the rich, memorable sauce; it is not a traditional Italian dish with one definitive origin.

Can I make it without sun-dried tomatoes?

You can, but the distinctive sweet-tart flavor will be reduced. Roasted red peppers are a possible alternative.

Can I freeze the sauce?

Cream sauces can change texture after freezing. Refrigerating leftovers and reheating gently is usually the better option.

Planning, texture, and serving notes

For the most consistent result, choose chicken breasts that are similar in size. If one end is much thicker than the other, gently flatten the thick portion or slice the breast horizontally to create even cutlets. This reduces the chance of a dry thin end and an undercooked center. Before adding the cream, take a moment to scrape the skillet well; the browned residue from searing is one of the most flavorful parts of the sauce.

The sauce should coat the back of a spoon but remain fluid enough to serve over a side dish. Parmesan thickens it as it melts, and it will tighten a little more while standing. Add cheese gradually over low heat and stir between additions. If it becomes too thick, loosen it with a tablespoon or two of broth. If the sauce tastes flat, first try a little basil or a few drops of lemon rather than adding more salt immediately.

This is a flexible centerpiece for a small dinner. For a casual meal, bring the skillet to the table with warm bread and a green salad. For a more filling plate, spoon the sauce over pasta or mashed potatoes and add steamed broccoli or green beans. Keep leftovers covered in the refrigerator and reheat gently; high heat can separate a cream sauce. The chicken should be heated through without allowing the sauce to boil aggressively.
