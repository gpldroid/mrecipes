Vegetarian Dinner · 30 minutes

Marry Me Chickpeas

Creamy Tuscan-style chickpeas simmered with sun-dried tomatoes, garlic, coconut milk and spinach. This comforting one-pan dinner brings bold, savory flavor to simple pantry ingredients.

Prep: 10 minCook: 20 minYield: 4 servings

At a glance: 30 minutes · 4 servings · one skillet · vegetarian and dairy-free when made with plant-based broth.

Some dinners earn a place in the weekly rotation because they ask very little of you and still feel special. Marry Me Chickpeas fit that brief: tender chickpeas are coated in a creamy tomato sauce, with sweet-tart sun-dried tomatoes, garlic, herbs and a handful of fresh spinach. The result is rich and comforting without requiring a long ingredient list or a complicated cooking method.

The dish takes its cue from the familiar “Marry Me” style of creamy tomato skillet recipes. Here, chickpeas take the lead, making the meal hearty and convenient. Coconut milk creates the silky texture while keeping this version free from dairy; vegetable broth, tomato paste and sun-dried tomatoes build a savory base with plenty of depth.

This is a useful recipe for busy evenings, meat-free meal planning and anyone who keeps canned beans in the cupboard. It is also flexible: serve it with bread to catch the sauce, spoon it over rice, or add a little pasta for a more substantial bowl.

Why you will love these Marry Me Chickpeas

  • One-pan cooking: The aromatics, sauce and chickpeas come together in one wide skillet, so cleanup stays manageable.
  • Everyday ingredients: Canned chickpeas, coconut milk, tomato paste and dried herbs are easy to keep on hand.
  • Big flavor, little effort: Sun-dried tomatoes bring concentrated sweetness and tang, while garlic and Italian seasoning make the sauce fragrant.
  • Easy to adapt: Adjust the chili or herbs, add extra greens, or choose a different bean if that is what you have.
  • Meal-prep friendly: The sauce keeps well and can be loosened with a splash of broth when reheated.

Ingredients

These quantities make four generous side portions or about three larger main-course servings. For four full dinner portions, serve the chickpeas with rice, bread or another filling side.

  • 1 tablespoon olive oil: Helps soften the onion and carry the flavor of the garlic and seasonings.
  • 1 small yellow onion, finely diced: Adds a mellow savory base. A shallot can be used instead.
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced: Fresh garlic gives the sauce its characteristic aroma.
  • 1/2 cup chopped sun-dried tomatoes: Use oil-packed tomatoes, drained, or rehydrate dry-packed tomatoes before chopping.
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste: Concentrates the tomato flavor and gives the sauce a deeper color.
  • 1 teaspoon Italian seasoning: A convenient blend of dried herbs. If yours is salt-free, adjust seasoning at the end.
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika: Adds gentle warmth and a subtle smoky note.
  • 1/2 cup low-sodium vegetable broth: Loosens the tomato paste and helps form the sauce. Use water if needed, then season to taste.
  • 1 cup unsweetened coconut milk: Choose canned, full-fat coconut milk for the creamiest result. Stir the can before measuring.
  • 2 cans (15 ounces / 425 g each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed: These are the hearty center of the dish. Drain them well so the sauce does not become watery.
  • 2 packed cups baby spinach: Stirred in at the end so it stays green and tender.
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt, plus more to taste, and black pepper: Start lightly because sun-dried tomatoes and broth can vary in saltiness.
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice: Brightens the creamy sauce just before serving.
  • Fresh basil or parsley: A fresh finish that balances the richness.

How to make Marry Me Chickpeas

  1. Prepare the ingredients. Dice the onion, mince the garlic and chop the sun-dried tomatoes. Drain and rinse the chickpeas, and measure the broth and coconut milk before heating the pan.
  2. Soften the onion. Warm the olive oil in a wide skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until it turns soft and translucent. Lower the heat if the edges begin to brown too quickly.
  3. Add garlic and tomato flavor. Stir in the garlic and cook for about 30 seconds. Add the sun-dried tomatoes, tomato paste, Italian seasoning and smoked paprika. Cook for 1 minute, stirring continuously so the tomato paste coats the onion without sticking.
  4. Build the creamy sauce. Pour in the vegetable broth and scrape the bottom of the skillet with a wooden spoon. Add the coconut milk and stir until the tomato paste is evenly incorporated. The sauce should look smooth and lightly orange-red.
  5. Simmer the chickpeas. Add the drained chickpeas, salt and several turns of black pepper. Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer, then cook uncovered for 10 to 12 minutes. Stir every few minutes. Avoid a hard boil, which can cause the coconut milk to separate and the sauce to reduce too quickly.
  6. Finish with spinach and lemon. Fold in the spinach and cook for 1 to 2 minutes, just until wilted. Turn off the heat, add the lemon juice and taste. Add more salt, pepper or lemon if the sauce needs balance.
  7. Garnish and serve. Scatter over chopped basil or parsley. Bring the skillet to the table while warm, with bread, rice or orzo for serving.

Tips for a creamy, flavorful sauce

  • Use gentle heat after adding coconut milk. A low, steady simmer helps the sauce thicken without becoming oily or splitting.
  • Let the tomato paste cook briefly. Stirring it with the aromatics for a minute removes its raw taste and adds a deeper savory note.
  • Choose unsweetened coconut milk. Avoid coconut drinks sold in cartons for this recipe; they are much thinner and may be sweetened.
  • Control the consistency. If the sauce becomes too thick, add broth one tablespoon at a time. If it is thin, simmer for a few extra minutes before adding the spinach.
  • Season at the end. Sun-dried tomatoes, broth and canned chickpeas vary in salt content. Taste before adding extra salt.
  • Keep the spinach fresh-looking. Add it only after the chickpeas are hot and the sauce has thickened.

Easy variations

Make it spicier: Add a pinch of red pepper flakes with the Italian seasoning. Start small and add more at the table.

Add more vegetables: Sliced mushrooms can be cooked with the onion, while chopped kale can replace spinach. If using kale, simmer it for a few extra minutes until tender.

Use a different bean: Butter beans or cannellini beans work well in place of chickpeas. Handle them gently when stirring so they keep their shape.

Make the flavor brighter: Add a little extra lemon juice or finish with fresh basil. For a more herb-forward sauce, add chopped parsley just before serving.

Adjust the richness: Use a little less coconut milk and more broth for a lighter sauce. The result will be less creamy but still flavorful.

What to serve with Marry Me Chickpeas

Crusty bread is the easiest pairing because it soaks up the tomato-coconut sauce. For a more complete meal, spoon the chickpeas over steamed basmati rice, couscous or orzo. A crisp green salad with lemon dressing adds freshness, while roasted vegetables make the plate more substantial. If you are serving several people, place extra herbs and lemon wedges on the table so everyone can finish their portion to taste.

Storage and reheating

Allow leftovers to cool, then transfer them to a covered container and refrigerate promptly. Enjoy within 3 to 4 days. Reheat gently in a saucepan over low to medium-low heat, stirring occasionally. Add a splash of water or vegetable broth if the sauce has thickened in the refrigerator. Heat until steaming throughout. Avoid repeatedly cooling and reheating the same batch; warm only the portion you plan to eat.

For freezing, cool the chickpeas completely and store in a freezer-safe container for up to 2 months for best quality. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator and reheat gently. Coconut-based sauces can change texture slightly after freezing; stirring while reheating and adding a little broth helps bring the sauce back together. Fresh spinach may soften more after thawing, so you can also freeze the chickpea sauce before adding spinach and stir fresh greens in during reheating.

Frequently asked questions

Are Marry Me Chickpeas vegan?

This version can be fully plant-based when you use vegetable broth and check that your sun-dried tomatoes and seasonings contain no animal-derived ingredients. It uses coconut milk instead of cream and does not require cheese.

Can I use dried chickpeas?

Yes. Cook dried chickpeas separately until tender before starting the skillet recipe. Do not add uncooked dried chickpeas directly to the sauce; they need sufficient soaking and cooking time. Use about 3 cups of cooked chickpeas in place of the two drained cans.

Can I replace coconut milk?

Unsweetened oat cooking cream or another neutral plant-based cooking cream can work, although the flavor and thickness will differ. If you use dairy cream, the recipe will no longer be dairy-free. Add alternative creams over gentle heat and avoid boiling vigorously.

How can I make the sauce thicker?

Simmer uncovered for a few extra minutes, or gently mash a small spoonful of chickpeas against the side of the skillet and stir them back in. This thickens the sauce without adding flour.

What if I do not have sun-dried tomatoes?

Chopped roasted red peppers can provide sweetness and color, but the sauce will taste milder and less concentrated. Add a little extra tomato paste and adjust lemon and salt to balance the flavor.
