Easy Shakshuka

Easy shakshuka simmers eggs in a spiced tomato and pepper sauce with cumin, paprika, and fresh herbs. Serve it straight from the skillet with warm bread.

Prep: 10 minCook: 25 minServes: 4 servings

Recipe at a glance: 35 minutes · 4 servings · Breakfast · Primary keyword: easy shakshuka recipe.

Easy shakshuka is a skillet of eggs gently cooked in a warmly spiced tomato and pepper sauce. It works for breakfast, brunch, or a simple supper, and most of the flavor comes from everyday pantry ingredients. Cumin and paprika make the sauce fragrant; optional chili brings heat without being essential.

The sauce needs time to reduce before the eggs are added. If it is too watery, the eggs spread out and the dish becomes harder to serve. Cook the onions and peppers until soft, briefly toast the spices, then simmer the tomatoes until thick enough to cradle the eggs in shallow wells.

Eggs can be cooked to different preferences, from soft yolks to fully set centers. Covering the skillet helps the tops cook through without needing to flip the eggs. Serve the pan at the table with warm bread, and add fresh herbs or feta at the end for contrast.

Ingredients

Prepare everything before turning on the stove. The ingredient list below matches the quantities used in the recipe instructions and structured recipe data.

  • 2 tbsp olive oil (30 ml)
  • 1 medium onion, thinly sliced (about 150 g)
  • 1 red bell pepper, diced (about 150 g)
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • 1/4 tsp chili flakes, optional
  • 800 g canned chopped tomatoes (about 28 oz)
  • 1/2 tsp salt, plus to taste
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • 4–6 large eggs
  • 30 g crumbled feta, optional
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley or cilantro
  • Warm crusty bread, to serve

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Warm olive oil in a wide skillet with a lid over medium heat. Add onion and bell pepper and cook for 6–8 minutes until softened and lightly golden.
  2. Add garlic, cumin, paprika, and chili flakes if using. Stir for 30 seconds until fragrant, taking care not to scorch the spices.
  3. Add tomatoes, salt, and pepper. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens enough to hold shallow wells.
  4. Use the back of a spoon to make 4–6 wells in the sauce. Crack an egg into each well, keeping yolks intact where possible.
  5. Cover the skillet and cook over low to medium-low heat for 5–8 minutes, checking near the end. Cook until whites are set; yolks may remain soft or cook through according to preference.
  6. Remove from the heat. Sprinkle with feta and herbs, then rest for 1 minute. The hot sauce will continue to cook the eggs slightly.
  7. Bring the skillet to the table and serve with bread for scooping up tomato sauce and egg.

Chef's tips for reliable results

  • Let the tomato sauce reduce before adding eggs; watery sauce makes the dish difficult to serve.
  • Make wells deep enough to cradle the eggs so they cook evenly.
  • Use a wide skillet so eggs have space and the sauce is not too deep.
  • For food safety, cook eggs until whites and yolks are firm when serving people at higher risk.

Easy variations and substitutions

  • Add chickpeas for a heartier vegetarian meal.
  • Stir chopped spinach into the sauce just before adding eggs.
  • For a North African-inspired heat, add harissa to taste; salt levels vary by brand.

What to serve with it

Choose sides that complement the dish instead of competing with it. A crisp green salad, steamed or roasted seasonal vegetables, or simply prepared grains can balance a rich main course. For pasta and soup, warm bread is useful for the sauce; for breakfast dishes and baking, serve with fresh fruit, yogurt, or a drink of choice. Add acidic elements such as lemon or lime at the table when the finished dish needs brightness.

Storage, reheating, and make-ahead notes

Cool leftovers promptly and refrigerate them in a clean, covered container. As a general guideline, cooked leftovers should be used within 3–4 days, unless an ingredient-specific food-safety recommendation calls for a shorter period. Reheat only the portion you plan to eat, until thoroughly hot. If the dish thickens in the refrigerator, add a small splash of water or broth while reheating. Do not leave perishable food at room temperature for extended periods.

For meal prep, measure dry seasonings and chop sturdy vegetables in advance. Keep raw meat or seafood separate from ready-to-eat ingredients, wash hands and utensils after handling them, and thaw frozen ingredients safely in the refrigerator or under cold running water. For best texture, add delicate herbs, greens, crunchy toppings, or final citrus just before serving.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping preparation: measure ingredients and complete chopping before cooking starts, especially for recipes that use high heat.
  • Overcrowding the pan: too many ingredients at once release moisture and can prevent browning. Work in batches if necessary.
  • Ignoring texture cues: use the suggested times as a guide, then check doneness, color, and consistency.
  • Seasoning only at the beginning: taste at the end and adjust salt, pepper, herbs, and acidity in small increments.
  • Storing food while very hot in a deep container: divide larger quantities into shallow containers so they cool more quickly and safely.

Frequently asked questions

Is shakshuka spicy?

It can be mild or spicy. Chili flakes and harissa are optional, so adjust them to your preference.

Can I make the sauce ahead?

Yes. Prepare and refrigerate the tomato-pepper base, then reheat and cook the eggs just before serving.

What should I serve with shakshuka?

Warm pita, crusty bread, or flatbread works well for scooping the sauce.

Planning, texture, and serving notes

Shakshuka is adaptable, but the tomato base should remain the star. Use a skillet wide enough for the eggs to sit apart, and simmer the sauce until it has lost its watery edge. If canned tomatoes are especially liquid, allow extra time for reduction before making the wells. If the sauce becomes too thick, add a small splash of water. Taste the sauce before adding eggs because seasoning is easier to correct at this stage.

Egg size changes how much space each one needs, so make the wells according to the eggs rather than trying to force a fixed pattern. Crack each egg into a small cup first if you want to avoid shell fragments and make placement easier. Covering the skillet traps heat and helps the whites set from above. Check the centers near the end because a minute can make the difference between runny and firm yolks.

Serve shakshuka directly from the skillet while the sauce is hot. Bread is practical for scooping, and a small bowl of olives or cucumber salad provides a refreshing contrast. If preparing the dish for guests, make the sauce in advance and reheat it before adding eggs. This avoids overcooking the eggs while waiting for everyone to sit down and keeps the texture closer to freshly made.
