Lemon Garlic Shrimp Pasta

Bright lemon, fragrant garlic, tender shrimp, and a silky olive-oil sauce make this lemon garlic shrimp pasta a quick, fresh dinner for busy evenings.

Prep: 10 minCook: 15 minServes: 4 servings

Recipe at a glance: 25 minutes · 4 servings · Dinner · Primary keyword: lemon garlic shrimp pasta.

Lemon garlic shrimp pasta is a quick dinner that relies on timing rather than a long ingredient list. Shrimp cook in minutes, garlic perfumes the olive oil, and lemon gives the pasta a clean finish. Instead of a heavy cream sauce, this recipe uses starchy pasta water to make a light emulsion that clings to the noodles.

The most important step is to have the ingredients ready before the shrimp hit the pan. Shrimp become rubbery if they are cooked too long, while garlic can turn bitter when browned. Cook the pasta just shy of fully tender, then finish it in the skillet so it absorbs the lemony oil and becomes evenly coated.

Use fresh lemon zest as well as juice for a fuller citrus aroma. Parsley adds freshness, and Parmesan is optional because seafood pasta traditions vary. A small amount of reserved pasta water can transform the pan juices into a glossy sauce; add it gradually instead of pouring it all in at once.

Why this lemon garlic shrimp pasta recipe works

This method separates the steps that build flavor from the steps that protect texture. Browning, aromatics, liquid, and finishing ingredients each have a clear role. Follow the order and use the sensory cues as well as the timer: ingredients vary in size, pans retain heat differently, and stovetops are not calibrated alike.

The quantities are provided in metric measurements with common US equivalents where useful. A kitchen scale gives the most consistent results, especially for flour, pasta, and produce. Salt brands and broth concentrations differ, so taste near the end and adjust gradually rather than adding extra seasoning automatically.

Ingredients

Prepare everything before turning on the stove. The ingredient list below matches the quantities used in the recipe instructions and structured recipe data.

  • 340 g linguine or spaghetti (12 oz)
  • 450 g raw shrimp, peeled and deveined (1 lb)
  • 3 tbsp olive oil (45 ml), divided
  • 5 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 1/4 tsp red pepper flakes, optional
  • 1 lemon, zest and 2 tbsp juice
  • 120 ml reserved pasta water, as needed (1/2 cup)
  • 30 g finely grated Parmesan, optional
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley
  • 3/4 tsp salt, divided
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil. Cook the pasta until just al dente according to the package directions. Reserve 1/2 cup pasta water before draining.
  2. While the pasta cooks, pat the shrimp dry and season with about half the salt and the black pepper.
  3. Warm 1 tablespoon olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add shrimp in a single layer and cook about 1–2 minutes per side, just until pink and opaque. Transfer to a plate; avoid overcooking.
  4. Lower heat to medium. Add the remaining olive oil, garlic, and red pepper flakes. Cook for 30–60 seconds, stirring constantly, until fragrant but not browned.
  5. Add lemon zest and juice plus 60 ml (1/4 cup) pasta water. Swirl the pan to create a light emulsion; add a little more pasta water if needed.
  6. Add drained pasta and toss vigorously for 1 minute so the sauce coats the strands. Return shrimp and any juices to the skillet and toss just until heated through.
  7. Remove from heat. Add parsley and Parmesan if using, taste, and adjust salt and lemon. Serve immediately with extra lemon wedges.

Chef's tips for reliable results

  • Do not rinse drained pasta; its surface starch helps the sauce cling.
  • Dry shrimp thoroughly for better searing and less watery sauce.
  • Garlic can turn bitter if it browns, so lower the heat before adding it.
  • Pasta water is the secret to a glossy sauce without heavy cream.

Easy variations and substitutions

  • Add halved cherry tomatoes to the garlic oil and cook until they soften.
  • Use whole-wheat linguine or gluten-free pasta, following the package cooking directions.
  • For a buttery finish, swirl in 1 tablespoon butter off the heat.

What to serve with it

Choose sides that complement the dish instead of competing with it. A crisp green salad, steamed or roasted seasonal vegetables, or simply prepared grains can balance a rich main course. For pasta and soup, warm bread is useful for the sauce; for breakfast dishes and baking, serve with fresh fruit, yogurt, or a drink of choice. Add acidic elements such as lemon or lime at the table when the finished dish needs brightness.

Storage, reheating, and make-ahead notes

Cool leftovers promptly and refrigerate them in a clean, covered container. As a general guideline, cooked leftovers should be used within 3–4 days, unless an ingredient-specific food-safety recommendation calls for a shorter period. Reheat only the portion you plan to eat, until thoroughly hot. If the dish thickens in the refrigerator, add a small splash of water or broth while reheating. Do not leave perishable food at room temperature for extended periods.

For meal prep, measure dry seasonings and chop sturdy vegetables in advance. Keep raw meat or seafood separate from ready-to-eat ingredients, wash hands and utensils after handling them, and thaw frozen ingredients safely in the refrigerator or under cold running water. For best texture, add delicate herbs, greens, crunchy toppings, or final citrus just before serving.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping preparation: measure ingredients and complete chopping before cooking starts, especially for recipes that use high heat.
  • Overcrowding the pan: too many ingredients at once release moisture and can prevent browning. Work in batches if necessary.
  • Ignoring texture cues: use the suggested times as a guide, then check doneness, color, and consistency.
  • Seasoning only at the beginning: taste at the end and adjust salt, pepper, herbs, and acidity in small increments.
  • Storing food while very hot in a deep container: divide larger quantities into shallow containers so they cool more quickly and safely.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use frozen shrimp?

Yes. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator or under cold running water, then drain and pat dry.

How can I tell shrimp are done?

They turn opaque and pink and curl into a loose C shape; overcooked shrimp become tight and rubbery.

Can I make it ahead?

This dish is best freshly made because shrimp and pasta can become firm when reheated.

Planning, texture, and serving notes

Choose shrimp of a similar size so they finish together. Very small shrimp can overcook while larger pieces are still translucent in the center. If using frozen shrimp, thaw them safely, drain well, and pat dry before seasoning. Wet shrimp lower the pan temperature and release liquid, which makes them steam instead of sear. Keep the lemon, parsley, and reserved pasta water ready before cooking because the finishing steps take only a minute.

The sauce is intentionally light, so it should not pool like a cream sauce at the bottom of the plate. Tossing pasta in the skillet helps starch from the noodles bind the olive oil, lemon juice, and pasta water into a glossy coating. Add water a tablespoon or two at a time and keep tossing. If the sauce becomes too loose, let it bubble gently for a few seconds; if it looks dry, add another splash of pasta water.

Serve this pasta as soon as it is finished, ideally in warmed bowls. A simple green salad, roasted asparagus, or steamed green beans makes a fresh side without competing with the lemon and garlic. Because seafood is delicate, leftovers are best limited and reheated gently. If you know you will need make-ahead portions, prepare the garlic-lemon base separately and cook the shrimp and pasta shortly before serving.
