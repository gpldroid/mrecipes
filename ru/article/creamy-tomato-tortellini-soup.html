Creamy Tomato Tortellini Soup

This creamy tomato tortellini soup combines a rich tomato broth, cheese-filled tortellini, spinach, and a splash of cream for an easy cozy meal.

Prep: 10 minCook: 25 minServes: 4–6 servings

Recipe at a glance: 35 minutes · 4–6 servings · Soup · Primary keyword: creamy tomato tortellini soup.

Creamy tomato tortellini soup combines the familiar comfort of tomato soup with cheese-filled pasta, greens, and a creamy finish. It is substantial enough for dinner while remaining simple to prepare in one pot. The key is to build a flavorful tomato base first and add the tortellini close to serving time.

Cooking tomato paste with the onion and garlic helps deepen its flavor and removes the raw, metallic edge. Crushed tomatoes and broth create the soup's body, while herbs add warmth. Cream rounds out the acidity, but the soup should simmer gently after it is added rather than boiling hard.

Tortellini brands vary in size and cooking time, so follow the package as the final guide. Refrigerated pasta usually cooks quickly, and it continues to soften as it sits in hot soup. If you are planning leftovers, cook the pasta separately and add it to individual bowls or to the pot just before serving.

Ingredients

Prepare everything before turning on the stove. The ingredient list below matches the quantities used in the recipe instructions and structured recipe data.

  • 1 tbsp olive oil (15 ml)
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced (about 150 g)
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste (30 g)
  • 800 g canned crushed tomatoes (about 28 oz)
  • 750 ml vegetable or chicken broth (3 1/4 cups)
  • 1 tsp dried basil
  • 1/2 tsp dried oregano
  • 250 ml heavy cream or half-and-half (1 cup)
  • 500 g refrigerated cheese tortellini (about 1 lb)
  • 100 g baby spinach (about 3 packed cups)
  • 40 g grated Parmesan, plus more to serve
  • 3/4 tsp salt, adjusted to taste
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Heat olive oil in a heavy soup pot over medium heat. Add onion and cook for 4–5 minutes until soft and translucent.
  2. Add garlic and tomato paste. Cook for 1 minute, stirring, until the paste darkens slightly and smells sweet rather than raw.
  3. Add crushed tomatoes, broth, basil, oregano, salt, and pepper. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce heat and simmer uncovered for 10 minutes to develop flavor.
  4. Stir in cream and bring the soup back to a gentle simmer. Avoid a hard boil after adding dairy.
  5. Add tortellini and cook according to package instructions, usually 3–7 minutes for refrigerated pasta. Stir occasionally to prevent sticking.
  6. Fold in spinach until just wilted. Stir in Parmesan and taste; add broth if the soup is thicker than you like, or a pinch of salt if needed.
  7. Ladle into warm bowls and top with extra Parmesan and black pepper. Serve with toasted bread for dipping.

Chef's tips for reliable results

  • Cooking tomato paste briefly deepens the soup's flavor.
  • Add tortellini near serving time; pasta continues to absorb liquid as it sits.
  • Use low-sodium broth so you can control the salt, especially with Parmesan and filled pasta.
  • For a smoother soup, blend the tomato base before adding cream and tortellini.

Easy variations and substitutions

  • Add drained white beans for extra body and plant-based protein.
  • Use kale instead of spinach, allowing a few extra minutes for it to soften.
  • For a lighter finish, use half-and-half; keep the heat gentle to reduce the chance of splitting.

What to serve with it

Choose sides that complement the dish instead of competing with it. A crisp green salad, steamed or roasted seasonal vegetables, or simply prepared grains can balance a rich main course. For pasta and soup, warm bread is useful for the sauce; for breakfast dishes and baking, serve with fresh fruit, yogurt, or a drink of choice. Add acidic elements such as lemon or lime at the table when the finished dish needs brightness.

Storage, reheating, and make-ahead notes

Cool leftovers promptly and refrigerate them in a clean, covered container. As a general guideline, cooked leftovers should be used within 3–4 days, unless an ingredient-specific food-safety recommendation calls for a shorter period. Reheat only the portion you plan to eat, until thoroughly hot. If the dish thickens in the refrigerator, add a small splash of water or broth while reheating. Do not leave perishable food at room temperature for extended periods.

For meal prep, measure dry seasonings and chop sturdy vegetables in advance. Keep raw meat or seafood separate from ready-to-eat ingredients, wash hands and utensils after handling them, and thaw frozen ingredients safely in the refrigerator or under cold running water. For best texture, add delicate herbs, greens, crunchy toppings, or final citrus just before serving.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping preparation: measure ingredients and complete chopping before cooking starts, especially for recipes that use high heat.
  • Overcrowding the pan: too many ingredients at once release moisture and can prevent browning. Work in batches if necessary.
  • Ignoring texture cues: use the suggested times as a guide, then check doneness, color, and consistency.
  • Seasoning only at the beginning: taste at the end and adjust salt, pepper, herbs, and acidity in small increments.
  • Storing food while very hot in a deep container: divide larger quantities into shallow containers so they cool more quickly and safely.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make tortellini soup ahead?

Prepare the tomato base ahead, then add tortellini and spinach when reheating for the best texture.

Can I use frozen tortellini?

Yes. Follow package timing and add a little extra broth if needed.

How long do leftovers keep?

Refrigerate promptly in a covered container and use within 3–4 days; the pasta will soften over time.

Planning, texture, and serving notes

The consistency of this soup is easy to adjust. Canned tomatoes vary in thickness, tortellini brands differ in size, and the pasta continues to absorb liquid after cooking. Keep a little extra broth nearby and use it to thin the soup if it becomes too dense. If you prefer a thicker bowl, simmer the tomato base for a few extra minutes before adding the tortellini rather than boiling the finished soup for a long time.

Choose cheese-filled tortellini that you enjoy on its own, because the filling is a prominent flavor in each spoonful. Fresh refrigerated tortellini usually cooks quickly; dried or frozen versions may need a different amount of time. Follow the package and taste a piece near the end. Add spinach only when the pasta is nearly done so the leaves stay green and do not become overly soft.

For meal preparation, make the tomato base with onion, garlic, tomatoes, broth, and herbs, then cool and refrigerate it separately. Reheat the base, add the cream, and cook the tortellini just before eating. This approach gives better texture than storing pasta in the soup for several days. Serve with toasted bread, a little Parmesan, and black pepper for a simple, complete-feeling meal.
